# Lesson 05 — Photographing a Hand So It Can Actually Be Read

> **Why this exists:** the first photo of a hand sent in for reading was unusable.
> Not because the hand was unusual, but because of how it was shot. This is the
> lesson that was missing — and it's the bridge between "find the lines on your own
> hand" and "have someone else read it."

---

## The standard your photo must meet

All five, or the reading is unreliable:

| # | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Palm flat against a plain surface** | Cupping fakes depth — shallow lines look dramatic |
| 2 | **Camera directly above, palm filling the frame** | Angles foreshorten creases and hide the true ending positions |
| 3 | **Light from the SIDE**, not overhead | Shadows reveal creases; overhead flash flattens them away |
| 4 | **Plain background** (white paper, dark cloth) | Clutter makes creases hard to trace |
| 5 | **Hand warm and dry** | Cold, damp hands show fainter lines than they really are |

## The retake checklist

Ask for these and the difference is night and day:

- Lay the hand **palm down on a table**, then shoot straight down, or palm up resting on the fingers
- **Elbow out, fingers relaxed and slightly spread** — not splayed in a claw
- **Kill the overhead light.** Put a lamp beside the hand, low, so each crease casts a small shadow
- Get the **whole hand in frame** — fingertips to just below the wrist
- If you can, take **both hands** in the same light, same distance
- Take it **sharp** — phone cameras need the hand to fill the frame, not sit small in a wide shot

## What went wrong with the first attempt

Recorded so it isn't repeated:

| Problem in the photo | Effect on the reading |
|---|---|
| Hand at a steep diagonal, camera off to one side | Every line's **ending position** — the most-read feature — became unreliable |
| Palm not flat, fingers loosely curled | Depth readings meaningless; creases foreshortened |
| Harsh overhead lighting, some flash | Fine lines washed out on one side |
| Cluttered background (papers, bottle) | Creases hard to trace and impossible to auto-trace |
| Ring/band crossing the thumb area | Obscured part of the Venus mount |

**Net result:** the Life, Head, and Heart lines were all *findable*, but their **endings could not be located with confidence** — and endings are where the meaning is. Attempting a full reading would have meant guessing, and guessing is the one thing the whole method is built to prevent.

## If an image is unusable — say so

The discipline that matters here: **an unreadable photo gets a request for a retake, not a confident reading.** Producing annotations on a bad image is worse than useless, because the learner can't tell the difference between a real observation and an invented one — and they learn the wrong thing either way.

Same principle as "describe before you interpret," applied to the medium instead of the hand.

## After this
Once there's a good photo of both hands, the reading can be done properly — and annotated, so you can see exactly which spot produces which meaning.
