# Hand Shapes & Skin Type

**One-sentence definition:** Before you read a single line, read the *shape* of the hand and the *texture* of the skin — the shape sets the baseline temperament, the texture adjusts how strongly to read everything else.

## Why it comes first
The shape is the frame the lines sit inside. Two people can have identical Life lines and completely different readings because one has a square palm and the other a long one. Every later interpretation is modified by shape.

## The four basic shapes

| Shape | How to spot | Palm ratio | Traditionally leans toward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earth** | square-ish, broad, thick fingers | wide, roughly as wide as long | practical, grounded, patient, steady — good with hands and real things |
| **Water** | wide palm, short tapered fingers, smooth skin | noticeably wide | feeling-led, intuitive, imaginative, mood-driven |
| **Air** | long narrow palm, long slender fingers, dry skin | longer than wide | intellectual, verbal, curious, needs mental stimulation |
| **Fire** | broad at the top, tapering to a narrow wrist, short fingers | narrow at wrist, "spade-like" | action-oriented, energetic, direct, improvisational |

**Mixes are the norm, not the exception.** Most hands are a blend — an "Earth-Fire" or "Water-Air." Only note the *dominant* shape and the one strong secondary, rather than forcing a pure label.

### What to actually observe
- **Width of palm at the knuckles** vs. **at the wrist** — this alone often decides Fire vs. the rest
- **Finger length relative to palm** — long fingers = Air/Water leaning; stubby = Earth/Fire
- **Taper** — fingers that taper sharply to a point read differently from ones that stay even
- **How the fingers are held** — splayed and relaxed vs. curled and controlled (observation, not doctrine, but palmists note it)

## Skin texture
The second half of the read, and the most directly observable:

| Texture | Traditionally reads as |
|---|---|
| **Smooth, fine, soft** | sensitivity, refinement, receptivity — emotions register easily |
| **Coarse, dry, cracked** | toughness, resilience, practical endurance; harder to knock down |
| **Elastic, pliable** | adaptability, flexibility, a flexible disposition |
| **Cold / clammy** | (classical) health-sign of circulation or nerve imbalance; many courses caution against reading health from skin at all |

## The line that matters most here
**Dry vs. oily skin is the most useful single observation** — it adjusts the *strength* of every line reading. Dry, fine skin reads lines as more sensitive/pronounced; thick, oily skin dampens them.

## ⚠️ Where traditions disagree
- **"Temperature" as a health sign** (hot = heart, cold = nerves) comes largely from classical Western text and is *not* supported by modern physiology. Most modern courses drop it. If your course teaches it, I'll record it — flagged as historical.
- **Whether shape is innate or environmental** — palmists vary. Note what *your* course says.
